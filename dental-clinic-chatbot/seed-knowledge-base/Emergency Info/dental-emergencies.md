# SmileCare Dental — Dental Emergency Information

> This information is general and is not medical advice or a diagnosis. If you have severe swelling, difficulty breathing or swallowing, uncontrolled bleeding, a high fever, or a serious facial injury, seek urgent or emergency medical care immediately (call your local emergency number).

## What counts as a dental emergency?
- Severe or worsening tooth pain
- A knocked-out (avulsed) tooth
- A broken, cracked, or chipped tooth causing pain
- Swelling of the face, gums, or jaw
- Bleeding that won't stop
- A lost filling or crown causing pain
- Signs of infection (swelling, fever, bad taste, pus)
- Injury to the mouth after an accident

## Same-day appointments
We reserve time each day for dental emergencies. If you contact us during business hours, we will do our best to see you the same day. Outside business hours, leave your details and our team will follow up as soon as we reopen.

## While you wait (general comfort tips, not a substitute for care)
- For a knocked-out tooth: handle it by the crown (not the root), gently rinse, and keep it moist in milk or saliva until you reach us.
- For swelling: a cold compress on the outside of the cheek may help.
- For pain: over-the-counter pain relief taken as directed on the package may help. Do not place aspirin directly on the gum.
